For each of the four [model and point triples](../CAS.md#inputs-and-executed-computations), the certificate proves

`H(m1 P1 + m2 P2 + m3 P3) ≥ (m1²+m2²+m3²)/100 ≥ max(|mi|)²/100`,

where `H(P)=lim_N 4^(-N) h(x([2^N]P))` is the **x-normalized** canonical height and `h(p/q)=log(max(|p|,q))` for coprime integers `p,q`, `q>0`; at the point at infinity, h is defined to be zero. This H is twice another common canonical-height normalization. The result concerns the subgroup generated by these three points. Extending it to all rational points requires the separate complete Mordell–Weil basis result; that result is now established by the separate [independent group proof](../descent/RANK_PROOF.md), not by this height script or a complete Lean endpoint.

## Inputs and independent arithmetic checks

`certify_height_intervals.py` uses only Python standard-library integers and `fractions.Fraction`. Its input is `duplication_bounds.json`, generated by the accompanying `duplication_bounds.py` using exact symbolic arithmetic. The script checks the listed points on their curves, computes the three pairwise sums exactly, and independently rechecks every coefficient of both supplied Bézout identities by integer polynomial convolution. It also reconstructs the duplication polynomials and checks the stated C and D coefficient norms. It does not use floating-point heights, eigenvalues, logarithms, or rank estimates.

For `y²=x³+a2*x²+a4*x+a6`, write `x=p/q`. The homogeneous duplication polynomials are

`F=p⁴−2a4*p²q²−8a6*pq³+(a4²−4a2a6)q⁴`,

`G=4p³q+4a2*p²q²+4a4*pq³+4a6*q⁴`.

To identify this pair with duplication, put `f(x)=x³+a2*x²+a4*x+a6`. Direct expansion gives

`f'(x)²−4f(x)(a2+2x)=F(x,1)`.

For y≠0, the tangent slope is `f'(x)/(2y)`, so the group law gives `x(2P)=F(x,1)/G(x,1)`. The two Bézout identities below, with R>0, exclude simultaneous projective zeros of F and G: otherwise they force both p⁷ and q⁷ to vanish. Thus a point with y=0 maps to infinity, and infinity itself maps to infinity because `F(1,0)=1`, `G(1,0)=0`. These checks of the connection to the elliptic group law have subsequently been formalized in `EllipticDuplication.lean`, including the exceptional points.

The supplied integer identities are `UF+VG=R*p⁷` and `U'F+V'G=R*q⁷`, where U,V,U',V' have degree 3 and R>0. Use primitive projective coordinates `(p:q)`; for infinity take `(1:0)`. Put `B=max(|p|,|q|)`, `K=max(|F|,|G|)`, and `d=gcd(F,G)`. For primitive p,q the two identities imply `d|R`. Taking the identity corresponding to a coordinate of size B gives `K≥R*B⁴/D`, where D bounds both coefficient norm sums. Thus `K/d≥B⁴/D`. The elementary coefficient upper bound is `K/d≤C*B⁴`. The same projective calculation handles the point at infinity, whose height is zero. Hence

`−log(D) ≤ h(x(2P))−4h(x(P)) ≤ log(C)`.

Telescoping, with `H_N(P)=4^(−N) h(x(2^N P))`, gives

`H_N(P)−log(D)/(3*4^N) ≤ H(P) ≤ H_N(P)+log(C)/(3*4^N)`.

This is the direction used in the script. In particular the error needed for the later elliptic-logarithm upper bound is `H−h≤log(C)/3`, not the bound on `h−H` returned by Magma's SilvermanBound.

## Certified logarithm intervals

For each positive integer t, the script writes t as `2^e*s`, with `1≤s<2`, and brackets s by its leading 60 binary digits. Endpoints lie in [1,2]. For a rational endpoint s, set `z=(s−1)/(s+1)`, so `0≤z≤1/3`. The identity

`log(s)=2*Σ_{j≥0} z^(2j+1)/(2j+1)`

has nonnegative terms. After T=40 terms, the remaining sum is between zero and

`2*z^(2T+1)/((2T+1)*(1−z²))`.

This bounds all later denominators below by `2T+1` and sums a geometric series. The same formula bounds log(2). Monotonicity, `log(t)=e*log(2)+log(s)`, and outward rounding to multiples of `2^−48` yield rational intervals. No transcendental approximation is assumed to be exact.

## Matrix certificate

With N=7, the program computes six height intervals on each curve: the three H(P_i) and the three H(P_i+P_j). Put

`Q_ii=H(P_i)`, `Q_ij=(H(P_i+P_j)−H(P_i)−H(P_j))/2`.

The canonical-height quadratic-form identity gives `H(Σ mi Pi)=mᵀQm`. Let B be the rational midpoint matrix, let r_ij be the half-widths, and let `rho=max_i Σ_j r_ij`. For every compatible symmetric Q,

`|mᵀ(Q−B)m|≤rho*Σ mi²`,

by `2|mi*mj|≤mi²+mj²`. The script checks that all three leading principal minors of

`B−(1/100+rho)I`

are strictly positive rational numbers. Sylvester's criterion then proves the stated lower bound. All four cases passed. Since H(O)=0, the inequality also proves that each displayed triple has no nonzero integer relation, independently of the Magma basis assertion; it does not prove full-group generation. The largest coordinate bit lengths after seven duplications were respectively 45575, 216285, 221662, and 101106.

## Theory and trust boundary

A primary exposition is J. S. Milne, [Elliptic Curves, second edition](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/EC2.pdf), Chapter IV: Lemmas 4.5–4.6 on printed p.124 give the canonical-height limit and geometric tail; Proposition 4.9 and Lemma 4.11 on pp.126–127 give the quadratic-form law. The book first uses short Weierstrass form. Our models transform to that form by `x'=x+a2/3`, `y'=y`. This changes naive height by a bounded amount: both directions give a projective-coordinate coefficient bound of 4 because `a2=±1`. Dividing that bound by `4^N` shows that the canonical limit is unchanged. The group isomorphism therefore transfers the quadratic-form law to these exact models.

The numerical logarithm enclosures, exact iterate data and matrix positivity are still justified by the written mathematics and independent Python certificate, outside the Lean kernel. Convergence, actual group height and the quadratic-form identity have subsequently been proved in Lean. The formal primitive-coordinate lower tail uses `log(32*R)`, whereas this script uses `log(D)`. For each of these four models `32*R<D`, so the formal lower enclosure is stronger and can conservatively imply the script's lower tail. This is distinct from the still sharper rational-point local gcd bounds needed for the separate global minimum-height proof. The numerical connection must still verify the iterate data, logarithms and matrix inequalities. Python's exact arithmetic remains outside the Lean kernel. No GRH, analytic-rank assumption, floating-point eigenvalue, or finite-search completeness assertion is used in this calculation.
